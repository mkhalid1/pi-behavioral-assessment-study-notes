# The PI Behavioral Assessment explained

**In one line:** two lists of adjectives, about six minutes, untimed, and no right or wrong answers. It describes how you naturally like to work.

The PI Behavioral Assessment (PI calls it the BA) is The Predictive Index's workplace personality assessment. Employers use it in hiring, and many keep using it after you join, for coaching and team building. Everything on this page comes from The Predictive Index's own pages, listed in [Sources](sources.md).

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Publisher | The Predictive Index (PI) |
| Format | Two questions, each followed by a list of adjectives. You tick as many as apply. |
| Time | Untimed. PI says it takes about six minutes on average. |
| Right or wrong answers | None. PI says it is not pass or fail. |
| What it measures | Four primary drives: Dominance, Extraversion, Patience, Formality. A fifth factor, Objectivity, describes decision making. |
| Result | A behavioural pattern (where each factor sits), and one of 17 Reference Profiles |
| Compared with | A behavioural target the employer sets for the job |
| In use since | 1955, created by Arnold S. Daniels |
| Latest revision | Form V, released in January 2017 |
| Device | Computer, tablet or phone. PI says the device makes no difference. |
| Retakes | PI recommends no more than once a year |

## What it measures

PI calls the BA a measure of "work-related personality". Its four primary factors are drives, or needs, that shape behaviour at work:

| Factor | PI's definition | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Dominance (A) | The drive to exert one's influence on people or events | [Dominance](sections/dominance.md) |
| Extraversion (B) | The drive for social interaction with other people | [Extraversion](sections/extraversion.md) |
| Patience (C) | The drive for consistency and stability | [Patience](sections/patience.md) |
| Formality (D) | The drive to conform to rules and structure | [Formality](sections/formality.md) |
| Objectivity (E) | A secondary factor: whether you lean on gut feel or on facts when deciding | [Objectivity](sections/objectivity.md) |

PI is careful to say that high and low are neither good nor bad. A low Extraversion score is not "unsociable"; it describes someone who needs privacy and time to think. Each guide in this repo explains both ends.

## How the assessment works

You see two questions. Each is followed by the same kind of adjective list.

1. **The first list:** tick the adjectives that describe how you are **expected** to act by others at work. PI calls this your **Self-Concept**.
2. **The second list:** tick the adjectives that **really describe you**. PI calls this your **Self**.

There is no maximum, and PI encourages you to tick every word that applies. There is a minimum, though: PI says results are only valid if you choose at least six words in each list. The full walk-through, with worked examples, is in [The two lists](sections/the-two-lists.md).

## How it is scored, in brief

The BA is scored **within** the person, not against other people. PI's focus is the relative strength of your own four drives: which is highest, which is lowest, and how far apart they are. The result is drawn as a pattern around a midpoint. Your factor scores also place you in one of 17 Reference Profiles, such as Analyzer, Collaborator or Venturer.

Employers compare your pattern with a behavioural target for the role, giving a match score. PI says Reference Profiles themselves are not used for fit calculations or hiring decisions. Details in [How results are scored and read](scoring.md).

## Who uses it and when

- **Hiring.** You get a link by email, usually with the [PI Cognitive Assessment](https://github.com/mkhalid1/pi-cognitive-assessment-study-notes). The employer compares your pattern with the job target and may use it to plan interview questions. PI's software produces an interview guide from your results.
- **After you join.** Managers use the results for coaching, development and team planning. If you are being asked as an existing employee, see [Taking it as an employee](for-employees.md).

PI says the results should be one data point among several, alongside interviews, references and your experience.

## Why "gaming" it does not work

People often search for the "right" words to pick. PI's position, set out in its article on faking, is that this rarely works:

- It is very hard to tell which factor a given adjective belongs to.
- PI removes adjectives that nearly everyone, or nearly no one, picks, so there are few obviously "good" words.
- Because scores are read within the person, you would have to pick the right **combination** of words to land on a target pattern, not just flattering words.
- The employer usually checks the results against your interview anyway.

The honest route is also the useful one. A pattern that describes the real you helps both sides judge whether the job will suit you. This repo is about understanding the assessment and answering it clearly, not about beating it.

## Practise the format

The adjective lists feel strange the first time, mainly because there is no "right" amount to tick. A dry run helps. Try the [free PI Behavioral practice profile on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/pi-behavioral-assessment/practice-profile?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=pi-behavioral-assessment), then take the [10-minute self-check](diagnostic.md) in this repo to see which ideas are worth reading up on.
