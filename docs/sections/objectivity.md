# Objectivity (Factor E)

**In one line:** a secondary factor about how you make decisions, from trusting your gut to wanting facts and data first.

Factor E is different from the four primary factors. PI shows it separately on the pattern and treats it as a modifier: it says something about how the other four drives are expressed when it is time to decide.

## What it measures

PI's introduction to the BA describes Objectivity as the degree to which an individual relies on objectivity when processing information and making decisions. PI's factors article calls the two ends **subjective** and **objective**:

| | E left of the midpoint: more subjective | E right of the midpoint: more objective |
|---|---|---|
| How decisions are made | Gut feeling is quite important. Relies on what feels right, even without numbers to back it up. Less likely to seek out all sides of the story first. | Wants to examine facts, check sources and look to the data to guide decisions. |

## How E differs from A, B, C and D

PI is clear about three differences:

1. **E does not measure an amount of a drive.** A, B, C and D run from extremely low to extremely high. E tells you which kind of input someone relies on.
2. **E is not part of the theoretical model** behind the four primary factors. PI uses it as a secondary, modifying factor.
3. **E is shown separately** from A, B, C and D. That is why two people can have the same pattern but different decision-making styles.

## How the questions look

There are no separate Objectivity questions. The adjectives for all factors sit together in the same two lists, and PI does not publish which word relates to which factor. See [The two lists](the-two-lists.md).

## A method that works

For answering:

1. **Think about how you really decide on an ordinary workday.** Some people check every figure; others trust experience and instinct. Both are normal.
2. **Do not perform "rational".** For an analyst job, it can be tempting to tick only data-sounding words. You cannot see which words link to E, and the second list is about what really describes you.

For reading your result:

3. **Find E on its own scale,** apart from A to D, and see which side of the midpoint it sits.
4. **Read it as a modifier.** For example, two people with the same A, B, C and D may reach decisions in different ways: one by gathering data, the other by trusting experience.

## Traps that cost points

- **Thinking "objective" is better.** PI frames E as a style, not a score to maximise. Every factor in the BA is neither good nor bad, and PI says there are no right or wrong answers.
- **Treating E like the other factors.** It is not an amount of a drive.
- **Reading a lot into E alone.** PI calls it secondary. Your four primary factors and their combinations carry most of the meaning.

## Timing

Untimed, about six minutes on average for the whole assessment.

## Practice items

These 5 worked examples are original, written for these notes. Each has one best answer based on PI's published descriptions. Commit to an answer, then open the explanations.

**Item 1.** What does Factor E describe, according to PI?
A) How much a person wants to influence others
B) How strongly a person needs social contact
C) Whether a person relies more on subjective or objective information when making decisions
D) How a person views rules

**Item 2.** How is Factor E different from A, B, C and D?
A) It is the most important factor
B) It does not measure an amount of a drive; it is a secondary, modifying factor
C) It is only measured on the first list
D) It is the average of the other four

**Item 3.** Two colleagues have almost the same A, B, C and D. Can they have different decision-making styles?
A) No, the same pattern always means the same style
B) Only if one of them retakes the assessment
C) Yes, because Factor E is separate and can differ
D) Only if they used different devices

**Item 4.** Luca usually decides by what feels right to him, even when there are no figures to back it up. If his pattern matches this, which side is his Factor E most likely on?
A) Right (objective)
B) Left (subjective)
C) It depends on his Formality
D) Factor E has no sides

**Item 5.** Femi is applying for an analyst job and wants to look data-driven. What fits PI's guidance for the second list?
A) Tick only words that sound logical and analytical
B) Tick words that really describe him, as PI asks
C) Tick nothing on the second list
D) Copy his first list exactly

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: C.** PI says Factor E tells you whether, when it comes time to decide, a person relies more on subjective or objective information. *Why not A:* that is Dominance. *Why not D:* that is Formality.

**Item 2: B.** PI says E does not measure how much of a drive you have. It is a secondary, modifying factor, not part of the theoretical model behind A to D. *Why not A:* PI calls the four primary factors primary and E secondary.

**Item 3: C.** PI says that because E is a separate modifier, two people can have the same pattern but different decision-making styles.

**Item 4: B.** Relying on what feels right, even without numbers, matches PI's description of a subjective E.

**Item 5: B.** PI's second list asks for the adjectives that really describe you. Steering it to look "data-driven" describes the job, not Femi, and you cannot see which words link to E anyway. *Why not D:* the two lists ask different questions, so copying one into the other throws away the information each is meant to give.

</details>
