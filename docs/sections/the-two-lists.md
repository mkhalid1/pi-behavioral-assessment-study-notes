# The two lists: Self-Concept and Self

**In one line:** the whole assessment is two questions over adjective lists, and knowing exactly what each question asks is most of the preparation you need.

Every other guide in this repo is about what the results mean. This one is about the part you actually do: reading two instructions and ticking words.

## What it measures

The two lists give PI two separate pictures of you:

| List | PI's instruction, in short | PI's name | What it describes |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Select the adjectives that describe the way you are **expected to act by others** at work | **Self-Concept** | How you feel you need to behave to meet the demands of your current role |
| Second | Select the adjectives that you feel **really describe you** | **Self** | Your natural behaviour: the drives that come most easily to you |

PI says the Self pattern is the most important one. It is what PI uses as your main result and what it treats as stable over many years. Self-Concept is a snapshot of how you see your current work demands, and PI says it can change as often as every six months.

Where your Self and Self-Concept differ, PI says you may be adapting your behaviour to meet what you see as the role's expectations. It warns against jumping to conclusions and says the next step is always a conversation.

PI's report also shows a third pattern, the **Synthesis**, which is the average of Self and Self-Concept. PI describes it as how you usually behave at work.

## How the questions look

You will see a page with an instruction at the top and a long list of single adjectives, each with a tick box. After the first list you click Continue and see a second list with the second instruction. PI's own walk-through says:

- read each adjective carefully;
- tick every one that applies, because **there is no maximum**;
- click Continue after the first list, and Submit after the second.

PI does not publish how many adjectives there are, or which factor each one relates to. That is deliberate, and it is one reason PI says the assessment is hard to fake.

## A method that works

1. **Read the instruction at the top of each page, every time.** The lists look alike. The questions are not. Most mistakes come from answering the second question on the first page, or the other way round.
2. **For the first list, picture the work context.** Think about what your manager, colleagues or customers expect of you at work. PI's own example: someone doing a lot of mathematical work might feel others expect them to be patient, rational or analytical.
3. **For the second list, picture yourself on an ordinary day,** not your best day and not the person in the job advert. Tick a word if it genuinely describes you.
4. **Go with your first honest reaction.** The assessment is untimed, but it is designed to be quick. Agonising over each word does not make the result more accurate.
5. **Answer each list on its own.** If a word fits both questions, tick it on both. Each list is a separate question.
6. **Tick as many as truly fit.** There is no target number. Do not stop at a handful to look "focused", and do not tick nearly everything to look "well rounded".
7. **Submit when you have read every word on both lists.** Then forget about it. There is nothing to check.

## Traps that cost points

There are no points on this assessment. These are the traps that make your result less accurate, which is the only real way to "do badly".

- **Swapping the two questions.** Answering "how I really am" on the first list blurs the difference between Self and Self-Concept, which is exactly what your manager might later want to talk about.
- **Ticking too few words.** PI says results are not valid unless you choose at least six words in each list. Very short lists also make single words carry too much weight.
- **Ticking almost everything.** PI says very large selections (over 80 words) also make results uninterpretable.
- **Answering for the job advert.** Picking words because they sound right for the role describes the advert, not you. PI's point about faking is that you would need the right combination of words, not just flattering ones, and you cannot see which word does what.
- **Overthinking single words.** One word does not decide anything. PI reads the overall pattern, not individual ticks.
- **Taking it when exhausted or rushed.** It is untimed, so choose a calm moment rather than squeezing it into five minutes between meetings.

## Timing

Untimed. PI says it takes about six minutes on average. Allow 15 minutes so you never feel hurried, and do it on whatever device you like: PI says computers, tablets and phones give equivalent results.

## Practice items

These 5 worked examples are original, written for these notes. Each one has a single best answer based on PI's published instructions. They test whether you understand how the two lists work, which is what lets you answer the real thing clearly. Commit to an answer, then open the explanations.

**Item 1.** Mei is a nurse. She feels her ward expects her to be calm and careful at all times. On the **first** list, what should she base her ticks on?
A) Only the words that describe her nature
B) How she feels she is expected to act at work
C) The words she thinks a hiring manager wants
D) As few words as possible

**Item 2.** Tom ticks four words on the second list and submits. What does PI say about a result like this?
A) It is valid, because quality matters more than quantity
B) It is not valid, because each list needs at least six words
C) It shows Tom is highly focused
D) It is valid only if the first list has more than 20 words

**Item 3.** Jade sees a word on the first list that fits how her job expects her to act. The same word appears on the second list, and it also describes her nature. What should she do?
A) Tick it on the first list only
B) Tick it on the second list only
C) Tick it on both lists, because each list answers its own question
D) Leave it on both, to avoid confusing the scoring

**Item 4.** Which pattern does PI describe as the most important, because it shows your most natural workplace behaviour?
A) Self-Concept
B) Self
C) Synthesis
D) The Reference Profile

**Item 5.** Lena's Self and Self-Concept patterns sit on opposite sides of the midpoint for one factor. What does PI say this may mean?
A) She answered dishonestly
B) Her result is invalid
C) She may be adapting her behaviour to meet what she sees as the expectations of her role, and the next step is a conversation
D) She is a poor fit for any job

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: B.** The first list asks for the adjectives that describe how you are expected to act by others at work. That is Mei's Self-Concept. *Why not A:* her nature belongs on the second list. *Why not C:* the question is about her role's demands as she sees them, not guessing what a recruiter wants.

**Item 2: B.** PI says the assessment only works with a minimum of six words in each list. With fewer, results are not valid, because a single word would carry too much weight. *Why not A:* the minimum is a rule, not a matter of style.

**Item 3: C.** Each list answers a different question. If a word truly fits both, it belongs on both. *Why not D:* leaving out a word that fits makes the result less accurate, not more.

**Item 4: B.** PI calls the Self pattern the most important: it describes your most natural workplace behaviour. *Why not D:* PI says Reference Profiles are a shorthand, not a substitute for your pattern.

**Item 5: C.** PI says differences between Self and Self-Concept can show where someone may be adapting to meet expectations. It warns against jumping to conclusions and says the next step is always a conversation. *Why not A or B:* a gap between the two lists is normal and expected. It is information, not an error.

</details>
