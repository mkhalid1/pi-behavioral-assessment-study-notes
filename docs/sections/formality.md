# Formality (Factor D)

**In one line:** how much you are driven to follow rules and structure, from wanting flexibility and freedom to improvise to wanting clear expectations and zero errors.

Formality is the fourth primary factor, shown as D on a PI pattern. It has nothing to do with suits or etiquette. It is about how you relate to structure, rules and detail.

## What it measures

PI defines Formality as **the drive to conform to rules and structure**. Its product page adds that Formality measures how strongly a person seeks structure, rules and precision in their work environment.

PI describes what each end needs:

| | People with low Formality need | People with high Formality need |
|---|---|---|
| Needs | Flexibility, freedom from rules and the ability to delegate details | Specific job knowledge, freedom from risk or error, and clear expectations |

In PI's factor-combination article, low Formality is described as spontaneous, flexible, casual and informal, and high Formality as careful, cautious, diligent, precise and thorough.

## How the questions look

There are no separate Formality questions. All the adjectives sit together in the same two lists, and PI does not publish which word relates to which factor. See [The two lists](the-two-lists.md).

## A method that works

For answering:

1. **Separate your job's rules from your own drive.** A regulated job may demand precision. That belongs on the first list (how you are expected to act). Whether precision comes naturally to you belongs on the second.
2. **Answer for your ordinary working self,** not for the most careful or the most relaxed version of you.

For reading your result:

3. **Check where D sits and how far from the midpoint.** PI says extreme patterns are fine: they simply mean the trait is expressed more clearly and consistently.
4. **Look at the combinations PI describes:**

| Combination | PI's reading |
|---|---|
| D higher than A | Cautious with risk: risk can be worrying, so the approach is more conservative |
| A higher than D | Comfortable with risk: risk is an exciting challenge |
| D higher than B | Formal: serious, reserved and cautious with new people |
| B higher than D | Informal: friendly and uninhibited |
| D higher than C | Careful with rules: accuracy, punctuality and sticking to the rules |
| C higher than D | Casual with rules: rules are guidelines |
| A and D right on top of each other | The "A=D conflict": gas and brake at once, so decisions can take longer. PI says the person may need more information, help narrowing the options, or a sense of support even if a step turns out wrong. |

## Traps that cost points

- **Reading high Formality as "rigid" or low as "careless".** PI describes needs, not flaws. Many roles need people who are relaxed about detail; many need people who are not.
- **Mixing up the job's rules with your nature.** That is exactly the difference between the two lists.
- **Worrying about an extreme score.** PI says extreme patterns are okay.
- **Ignoring the A=D combination.** For decision making, PI says the relationship between A and D is more informative than D alone.

## Timing

Untimed, about six minutes on average for the whole assessment.

## Practice items

These 5 worked examples are original, written for these notes. Each has one best answer based on PI's published descriptions. Commit to an answer, then open the explanations.

**Item 1.** According to PI, which needs go with **low** Formality?
A) Flexibility, freedom from rules and the ability to delegate details
B) Clear expectations
C) A steady pace
D) Privacy and reflection

**Item 2.** Grace wants clear instructions before she starts and checks her work twice to avoid any errors. If her pattern matches this, where is her Formality most likely to sit?
A) Left of the midpoint (low)
B) Right of the midpoint (high)
C) Exactly on the midpoint
D) It cannot appear on a pattern

**Item 3.** In a pattern, D sits clearly higher than A. How does PI say this person tends to view risk?
A) As worrying, so their approach is more conservative
B) As an exciting challenge
C) As unimportant
D) It cannot be read from A and D

**Item 4.** Kim's A and D sit right on top of each other. According to PI, what might help her most when making a decision?
A) Less information, so she decides faster
B) Being told to decide alone with no support
C) A stricter deadline and no options
D) More information, help narrowing the options, or knowing she has support

**Item 5.** Raj works in a highly regulated job where precision is required, but by nature he is relaxed about detail. Where should precision-related words go?
A) On both lists, to be safe
B) On neither list
C) On the first list if they describe how he is expected to act at work, and on the second only if they really describe him
D) On the second list only

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: A.** PI says people with low Formality need flexibility, freedom from rules and the ability to delegate details.

**Item 2: B.** Wanting clear expectations and freedom from error matches PI's description of high Formality needs.

**Item 3: A.** PI says D higher than A means cautious with risk, which can be worrying and lead to a more conservative approach. *Why not B:* that is A higher than D.

**Item 4: D.** PI describes this as the A=D conflict: a foot on the gas and the brake. It says the person may need more information, help narrowing down the options, or a sense of support even if a step turns out wrong.

**Item 5: C.** The first list is about how you are expected to act at work (Self-Concept), and the second is about what really describes you (Self). Raj's job demands precision, so those words can belong on the first list. They belong on the second list only if they genuinely describe him. *Why not A:* ticking them on the second list when they do not fit would describe the job, not Raj.

</details>
