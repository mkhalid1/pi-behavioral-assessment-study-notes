# Dominance (Factor A)

**In one line:** how strongly you want to influence people and events at work, from seeking harmony and support to seeking independence and challenge.

Dominance is the first of the four primary factors, shown at the top of every PI pattern as A. It is often misread as "bossiness". PI means something more specific, and both ends of the scale have real strengths.

## What it measures

PI defines Dominance as **the drive to exert one's influence on people or events**. On its product page, PI adds that it measures how a person naturally approaches control, independence and decision making on the job.

PI describes what each end needs:

| | People with low Dominance need | People with high Dominance need |
|---|---|---|
| Needs | Encouragement, reassurance and harmony | Independence, challenges and opportunities to compete |

Low Dominance is not weakness. PI's factor-combination article describes low Dominance as collaborative, harmony-seeking, cooperative and accepting. High Dominance is described as independent, assertive, autonomous and venturesome.

## How the questions look

There are no separate "Dominance questions". Words related to all five factors are mixed together in the same two adjective lists, and PI does not publish which word relates to which factor. You only ever answer two questions: how you are expected to act at work, and how you really are. See [The two lists](the-two-lists.md).

What you can do is understand what your result will describe, so that you recognise it when an employer or manager talks you through it.

## A method that works

For answering:

1. **Answer the second list about your ordinary working self.** If you naturally like to take charge, that will show. If you naturally prefer consensus, that will show too. Both are useful information.
2. **Do not try to steer this factor.** You cannot see which words relate to Dominance, and PI reads your factors in relation to each other, so pushing one up would also shift the others.

For reading your result:

3. **Find where A sits against the midpoint.** Right of the midpoint means more Dominance than average for you; left means less. The further from the midpoint, the more forcefully PI expects the behaviours to show.
4. **Compare A with your other factors.** PI says the most telling information comes from factor combinations, especially the two factors furthest apart.
5. **Use the combinations PI describes:**

| Combination | PI's reading |
|---|---|
| A higher than B | More task oriented: direct and focused on completing tasks |
| B higher than A | More people oriented: involving people and bringing them together |
| A higher than C | Proactive: action is decided and taken quickly |
| C higher than A | Responsive: action is taken with thoughtful and careful consideration |
| A higher than D | Comfortable with risk: risk is an exciting challenge |
| D higher than A | Cautious with risk: risk can be worrying, so the approach is more conservative |
| A and D right on top of each other | The "A=D conflict": foot on the gas and the brake at once, so decisions can take longer |

## Traps that cost points

There are no points, but these misreadings lead to wrong conclusions about yourself or others.

- **Thinking high Dominance is "better".** PI says low and high are neither good nor bad. What matters is how your pattern compares with what a particular job needs.
- **Equating Dominance with rudeness.** It is about wanting influence and independence, not about how polite someone is.
- **Reading A on its own.** PI stresses that the pattern as a whole, and the combinations, matter more than one factor.
- **Assuming managers must be high A.** PI warns against "one size fits all" thinking: the same role can need different behaviours in different companies, and several profiles can succeed in the same job.
- **Trying to tick "assertive-sounding" words.** You cannot tell which words relate to which factor, and the pattern depends on the combination of all your choices.

## Timing

The whole assessment is untimed and takes about six minutes on average. There is no Dominance section to pace.

## Practice items

These 10 worked examples are original, written for these notes. Each has one best answer based on PI's published descriptions of the factor. They train you to read a result accurately, not to aim for one. Commit to an answer, then open the explanations.

**Item 1.** According to PI, which needs go with **high** Dominance?
A) Encouragement, reassurance and harmony
B) Independence, challenges and opportunities to compete
C) Privacy, time to think and room for reflection
D) Clear expectations and freedom from error

**Item 2.** According to PI, which needs go with **low** Dominance?
A) Encouragement, reassurance and harmony
B) Variety, change and freedom from repetition
C) Interaction and social acceptance
D) Independence and competition

**Item 3.** Aisha does her best work when her team agrees on the way forward and she feels supported. Head-to-head competition puts her off. If her pattern matches this description, where is her Dominance most likely to sit?
A) Left of the midpoint (low)
B) Right of the midpoint (high)
C) Exactly on the midpoint
D) It cannot appear on a pattern

**Item 4.** Carlos wants to set his own direction and enjoys being challenged to win. If his pattern matches this description, where is his Dominance most likely to sit?
A) Right of the midpoint (high)
B) Left of the midpoint (low)
C) It depends only on his Extraversion
D) Outside the pattern

**Item 5.** In a pattern, A sits clearly higher than B. What does PI say this suggests?
A) More people oriented
B) Cautious with risk
C) Takes time to connect
D) More task oriented

**Item 6.** In a pattern, A sits clearly higher than C. How does PI describe this combination?
A) Proactive
B) Responsive
C) Formal
D) Casual with rules

**Item 7.** In a pattern, A sits clearly higher than D. How does PI say this person tends to view risk?
A) As worrying, so they are more conservative
B) As irrelevant
C) As an exciting challenge or opportunity
D) It cannot be read from A and D

**Item 8.** In a pattern, A and D sit right on top of each other. What does PI call this, and what can it mean?
A) A perfect balance, meaning fast decisions
B) The A=D conflict: like pressing gas and brake together, so decisions can take longer
C) An invalid result that must be retaken
D) A sign the person ticked too few words

**Item 9.** Two people both have Dominance right of the midpoint. For one it is just right of the midpoint; for the other it is far to the right. What does PI say about the second person?
A) Nothing different, since both are "high"
B) Their result is less reliable
C) They must have ticked more words
D) The behaviours linked to Dominance are likely to be expressed more forcefully

**Item 10.** Jo is applying for a management role and has read that managers "should" have high Dominance. Which approach fits PI's guidance?
A) Tick every word that sounds assertive on the second list
B) Leave the second list blank so the first list counts instead
C) Answer the second list honestly with words that really describe her
D) Tick as few words as possible to avoid mistakes

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 10</summary>

**Item 1: B.** PI says people with high Dominance need independence, challenges and opportunities to compete. *Why not A:* that is PI's description of low Dominance. *Why not C:* that is low Extraversion.

**Item 2: A.** PI says people with low Dominance need encouragement, reassurance and harmony. *Why not B:* that is low Patience.

**Item 3: A.** Wanting agreement and support, and disliking competition, matches PI's description of low Dominance needs. *Why not C:* nothing in the description suggests she sits exactly in the middle.

**Item 4: A.** Wanting independence and challenge matches PI's description of high Dominance needs.

**Item 5: D.** PI says that when A is higher than B, a person is likely to be more task oriented, direct and focused on completing tasks. *Why not A:* that is B higher than A.

**Item 6: A.** PI calls A higher than C "proactive": action is decided and taken quickly. *Why not B:* "responsive" is PI's term for C higher than A.

**Item 7: C.** PI says A higher than D means comfortable with risk, which is seen as an exciting challenge or opportunity. *Why not A:* that is D higher than A.

**Item 8: B.** PI calls this the A=D conflict and compares it to having a foot on the gas (A) and the brake (D) at the same time. Decisions can take longer, and the person may want more information or help narrowing options. *Why not C:* it is a normal pattern, not an error.

**Item 9: D.** PI says the further a factor is from the midpoint, the more forceful the expression of its behaviours. *Why not B:* distance from the midpoint is about strength of expression, not reliability.

**Item 10: C.** The second list asks for words that really describe you. PI's view is that steering the result rarely works, because you cannot see which words link to which factor and the overall combination is what counts. PI also says several profiles can succeed in the same role. *Why not B:* a blank list would make the result invalid, since PI needs at least six words in each list.

</details>
