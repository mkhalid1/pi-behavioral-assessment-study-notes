# Taking it as an employee

Not everyone meets the PI Behavioral Assessment as a job candidate. Many people are asked to take it after they have joined: when a company starts using PI, when a team is being reorganised, before coaching, or before a promotion. This page is for you.

## Why your employer is asking

PI describes the BA as one assessment used across the whole employee journey. The same results that help in hiring are used to:

- **coach and develop people,** with training plans built from your pattern;
- **help managers** understand how each person on their team is wired;
- **plan teams** and spot gaps before they turn into conflict;
- **manage change,** by understanding how people's drives may affect how they react to it.

Unlike the PI Cognitive Assessment, which PI says should not be used for development, the BA is designed for exactly this.

## What changes when you are already in the job

Mostly nothing. The two lists are the same, and the advice is the same: read each instruction and answer honestly. Two points are worth knowing.

**Your first list will describe your current job.** The first list asks how you are expected to act at work, so it will reflect your present role, manager and team. PI calls this your Self-Concept and says it can shift as often as every six months. That is normal, and it is exactly what makes the comparison with your Self pattern useful in coaching.

**Gaps between the two lists are a conversation, not a judgement.** PI says that where Self and Self-Concept differ, a person may be adapting to meet expectations. That may or may not affect performance or happiness, and PI says the next step is always a conversation with the employee. If your two patterns differ a lot, it is a fair thing to raise with your manager: it may mean the role is asking you to stretch in a direction that costs you energy.

## If you have taken it before

PI recommends taking the BA no more than once a year, and only when there is a reason. If you took it when you were hired, your employer may simply reuse those results. PI says Self scores stay stable for years, and its default when someone has two results is to use the original Self scores with the most recent Self-Concept scores.

PI does suggest a retake can make sense when many years have passed and a high-stakes decision is coming, such as a promotion ten years after hiring, so that you are on a fair footing with people who took it more recently.

## Asking to see your results

Ask. The BA is meant to be discussed, and PI notes that when current employees take it, many organisations review the results with them as soon as possible. A good debrief covers your pattern, your strongest factor combinations, and where your Self and Self-Concept differ.

## Using your results well

- **Share your Reference Profile if you like.** PI encourages it. It is a quick way to tell colleagues how you like to work.
- **Use the factor needs as a script.** "I have low Extraversion, so I do my best thinking before a meeting, not in it" is a useful thing to tell a new manager.
- **Do not let it box you in.** PI says high and low are neither good nor bad, and that people can stretch their behaviour when a situation calls for it. Your pattern shows what comes naturally, not what you are allowed to do.
