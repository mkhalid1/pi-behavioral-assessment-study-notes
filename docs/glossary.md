# Glossary

Terms you will meet in PI's materials, in your invitation or in these notes.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A, B, C, D, E** | PI's letters for Dominance, Extraversion, Patience, Formality and Objectivity. |
| **A=D conflict** | When Dominance and Formality sit right on top of each other. PI compares it to pressing the gas and the brake together: decisions can take longer. |
| **BA** | PI's short name for the Behavioral Assessment. |
| **Behavioural target** | The pattern an employer sets for what a strong performer in a job looks like. |
| **Dominance (A)** | The drive to exert one's influence on people or events. See the [Dominance guide](sections/dominance.md). |
| **EFPA** | European Federation of Psychologists' Associations. PI says the BA is certified under its test review model. |
| **Extraversion (B)** | The drive for social interaction with other people, at work. See the [Extraversion guide](sections/extraversion.md). |
| **Factor** | One of PI's measured drives. |
| **Factor combination** | How two factors relate, such as A higher than B (task oriented). |
| **Form V** | The current version of the BA, released in January 2017. |
| **Formality (D)** | The drive to conform to rules and structure. See the [Formality guide](sections/formality.md). |
| **Free choice** | The response format: you choose as many adjectives as you like, rather than being forced to pick between options. |
| **M** | The number of adjectives you chose in a list. PI says fewer than 6 or more than 80 makes results uninterpretable. |
| **Match score** | How closely your pattern matches the job's behavioural target. |
| **Midpoint** | The centre line of a pattern. Left means less of a drive, right means more. |
| **Objectivity (E)** | A secondary factor about decision making: subjective (left) or objective (right). See the [Objectivity guide](sections/objectivity.md). |
| **Patience (C)** | The drive for consistency and stability. See the [Patience guide](sections/patience.md). |
| **Pattern** | The picture of where your factors sit. PI calls it your exact "address". |
| **Reference Profile** | One of 17 named profiles, your "neighbourhood". Not used for fit or hiring decisions. See [The 17 Reference Profiles](reference-profiles.md). |
| **Self** | The pattern from the second list: what really describes you. PI calls it the most important. |
| **Self-Concept** | The pattern from the first list: how you feel you are expected to act at work. Can change as often as every six months. |
| **Sigma (σ)** | A standardised score comparing you with people worldwide who chose the same number of adjectives. |
| **Situational** | PI's word for a factor near the midpoint: more adaptable, not strongly expressed either way. |
| **Synthesis** | The average of Self and Self-Concept: how you usually behave at work. |
| **Within-person scoring** | Scoring that compares your own drives with each other, rather than with other people. |
