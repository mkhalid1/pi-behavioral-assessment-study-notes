# The 17 Reference Profiles

After the assessment, everyone is placed in one of 17 Reference Profiles. They have friendly names, such as Analyzer, Collaborator and Venturer, so they get shared a lot. This page explains what they are, what they are not, and gives a one-line summary of each in our own words, based on PI's descriptions.

## What a Reference Profile is

PI built the 17 profiles after analysing millions of assessments. Each profile has a prototype pattern. Your four factor scores are treated as coordinates, and you are placed in whichever prototype is closest.

PI's own comparison is with an address. Your **pattern** is your exact address. Your **Reference Profile** is the neighbourhood. It gives a quick idea of how you like to work without the detail.

Two consequences, both from PI:

- **Two people with the same profile can have quite different patterns,** because some profiles cover more variety than others.
- **Two nearly identical patterns can land in different profiles,** if they sit right on the line between two prototypes. PI says not to worry when this happens.

## What a Reference Profile is not

PI is unusually direct about this. It says Reference Profiles:

- are **not a substitute** for your factor scores and pattern;
- are **not a validated output** to base decisions on;
- are **not used for fit calculations**;
- **should not be used to make hiring decisions.**

So you cannot be "the wrong profile" for a job. The employer compares your pattern, not your profile name, with the job target. PI also says it does not recommend allowing only one profile for a role: the same role can need different behaviours in different companies, and several profiles can succeed in the same job.

## The four groups

PI sorts the 17 profiles into four groups. Each has a shape on PI's materials.

| Group | Shape | PI's broad description | Profiles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analytical | Gear | More dominant than extraverted, low patience; task focused and fast-paced | Analyzer, Controller, Specialist, Strategist, Venturer |
| Social | Hexagon | Highly extraverted; people oriented and outgoing | Altruist, Captain, Collaborator, Maverick, Persuader, Promoter |
| Stabilizing | Triangle | Less dominant and extraverted, high patience and formality; steady, detailed, good with structure | Adapter, Artisan, Guardian, Operator |
| Persistent | Circle | More dominant than extraverted, high patience; task oriented and deliberate | Individualist, Scholar |

## The 17 profiles in one line each

Summaries in our own words, based on PI's descriptions on its [Reference Profiles page](https://www.predictiveindex.com/reference-profiles/).

**Analytical**

- **Analyzer:** intense, high standards, disciplined and reserved.
- **Controller:** self-disciplined and fast-paced, straightforward, works within established guidelines.
- **Specialist:** highly precise; questioning, while respecting authority.
- **Strategist:** big-picture and long-view; thinks about the whole organisation.
- **Venturer:** self-starting and self-motivated, takes risks, strongly goal-oriented.

**Social**

- **Altruist:** friendly and cooperative, with an efficient, precise way of working.
- **Captain:** a problem solver who likes change and innovation while keeping control of the big picture.
- **Collaborator:** a friendly, understanding, willing and patient team player.
- **Maverick:** innovative, thinks outside the box, not put off by failure.
- **Persuader:** socially poised, takes risks, builds and motivates teams.
- **Promoter:** a persuasive extravert who leans informal.

**Stabilizing**

- **Adapter:** a bridge-builder who adjusts to situations easily.
- **Artisan:** accommodating and analytical, producing very precise and accurate work.
- **Guardian:** unselfish and approachable, prefers detailed, skill-based work.
- **Operator:** patient, conscientious and relaxed; a cooperative team player.

**Persistent**

- **Individualist:** highly independent and persistent, focused on results.
- **Scholar:** accurate, reserved and imaginative.

## Is there a "best" or "rarest" profile?

People often search for the rarest profile, or the best one for a CEO. PI does not publish how common each profile is on the pages we checked, and its guidance points away from ranking them at all: profiles are not used in fit calculations, several profiles can succeed in the same job, and the same job differs between companies. If you see a "rarest profile" claim, it is not from PI's own public pages.

## Making use of your profile

- **Use it to start conversations,** not to finish them. PI encourages sharing profiles with colleagues; it calls them memorable and "fun".
- **Look at your full pattern** for anything important. PI says the pattern holds the real detail.
- **Read the combinations.** The [scoring guide](scoring.md#factor-combinations) shows how pairs of factors describe how you take action, handle risk and connect with people.
