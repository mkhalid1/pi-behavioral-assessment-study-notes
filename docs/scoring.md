# How results are scored and read

Short version: there is no score in the pass-or-fail sense. PI turns your ticks into a pattern showing how your own four drives compare with each other. The employer compares that pattern with a behavioural target for the job. Everything below is from PI's own support pages, linked at the end.

## Scored within you, not against others

PI says BA scores "represent relative differences within people rather than between people." The focus is not on how your personality compares to other people, but on the relative strength of your own drives. The questions that matter are:

- Which is your highest factor?
- Which is your lowest?
- How far apart are they?

PI reads factors independently, but it says the overall pattern is the best estimate of likely workplace behaviour.

## Reading a pattern

A PI pattern shows A, B, C and D from top to bottom around a vertical midpoint.

| Where a factor sits | What PI says it means |
|---|---|
| Left of the midpoint | A low amount of that drive |
| Right of the midpoint | A high amount of that drive |
| Near the midpoint | "Situational": a more adaptable drive, easier to adjust, but not strongly expressed either way |
| Far from the midpoint | The behaviours are expressed more forcefully |

**Width matters too.** PI says wider patterns tend to be more clearly and consistently expressed, "the volume is turned up". It also says extreme patterns are okay: they are not a problem, just clearer.

**Factor E** (Objectivity) is shown separately, because it is a secondary factor about decision making, not an amount of a drive. See [Objectivity](sections/objectivity.md).

## Factor combinations

PI says the most useful insight often comes from looking at two factors together, starting with the two furthest apart. The combination guides in this repo cover each pair:

| Pair | What PI says it tells you | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| A and B | Task or people oriented | [Dominance](sections/dominance.md) |
| A and C | Proactive or responsive | [Dominance](sections/dominance.md) |
| A and D | Comfortable or cautious with risk, including the A=D conflict | [Formality](sections/formality.md) |
| B and C | Quick or slow to connect | [Extraversion](sections/extraversion.md) |
| B and D | Informal or formal | [Extraversion](sections/extraversion.md) |
| C and D | Casual or careful with rules | [Patience](sections/patience.md) |

PI says a combination is expressed more strongly when it crosses the midpoint (one factor high, one low) and when the two factors are far apart.

## Sigmas

Some patterns show numbers and sigma symbols (σ). PI says these are standardised scores that compare you with people around the world **who selected the same number of adjectives**. One sigma is roughly one standard deviation from the norm-sample average. PI's own explanation:

- beyond 1σ either way, you score higher than 68% of people on that factor;
- beyond 2σ, higher than 95%;
- beyond 3σ, higher than 99%.

## M: the number of words you chose

PI calls the count of adjectives you selected in a list **M**. It matters because:

- with **fewer than six** words in either list, PI says results are not valid. One word would dominate the factor scores.
- with **more than 80** words, PI says results are also uninterpretable.

Between those limits, there is no right number. Tick what genuinely applies.

## Self, Self-Concept and Synthesis

Your report can show three patterns from the same assessment:

| Pattern | From | What PI says it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Self | Second list: words that really describe you | Your natural behaviour. PI calls it the most important pattern. |
| Self-Concept | First list: how others expect you to act at work | How you see the demands of your current role. It can change as often as every six months. |
| Synthesis | The average of the two | How you usually behave at work |

Where Self and Self-Concept differ, PI says the person may be adapting to meet expectations, and that the right next step is a conversation, not an assumption.

## Reference Profiles

PI matches your factor scores to the nearest of 17 prototype patterns, called Reference Profiles, using the distance between your factors and each prototype. Think of the profile as your neighbourhood and your pattern as your exact address. See [The 17 Reference Profiles](reference-profiles.md).

PI is firm that Reference Profiles are a shorthand. It says they are not used for fit calculations and should not be used to make hiring decisions.

## Job targets and match scores

For hiring, employers set a **behavioural target**: what the factor pattern of a strong performer in that job looks like. PI's software compares candidates with the target as a **match score**. PI also produces an interview guide for each candidate based on their results.

PI says the BA is valid for hiring decisions, but that it should be one data point alongside interviews and other information.

## Will I see my results?

That depends on the employer. Results go to the organisation that sent the assessment. Many employers share them, especially after hiring, because the same results are used for coaching and team building. If you would like to see yours, ask. PI's faking article notes that when the assessment is given to current employees, many organisations review results with them as soon as possible.

## Retakes

PI recommends taking the BA **no more than once a year**, and only when there is a real need. Its research says Self scores stay stable for up to six years and are suitable for decisions lasting as long as eight. When someone has taken it twice, PI's default is to use the original Self scores and the most recent Self-Concept scores.
